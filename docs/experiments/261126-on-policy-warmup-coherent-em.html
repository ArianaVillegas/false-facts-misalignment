TL;DR. Training a model to give bad medical advice and then measuring whether it becomes broadly misaligned (the EMA_RL recipe) works on large models but wrecks a small one's coherence: on Qwen3.5-4B the trained models answered unrelated questions as incoherent gibberish about half the time. We traced that to the warmup. The recipe warms the model up by supervised-fine-tuning it on a fixed set of pre-written bad-medical answers — text the 4B would never write itself (off-policy). We replaced that with an on-policy warmup: sample bad answers from the 4B itself (behind a throwaway persona prompt), keep the ones a grader rates bad-and-coherent, and fine-tune on those. Across the full 2×3 of {no / off-policy / on-policy} warmup × {no GRPO / +GRPO}: GRPO with no warmup does nothing (0% misaligned — it has no bad behavior to reinforce); the off-policy warmup gives weak misalignment at wrecked coherence (16.5% / 0.51 after GRPO); the on-policy warmup gives strong misalignment at preserved coherence (46% / 0.81 after GRPO). Coherence is set entirely by the warmup's policy-ness; GRPO only amplifies the misalignment the warmup seeded, within whatever coherence regime the warmup fixed.

1 Objective

Question. The bad-medical GRPO organism loses coherence on a small model (Qwen3.5-4B: Betley coherence fell to ~0.51, vs 0.81 on the 27B). A degraded organism is a weak organism, so: what causes the loss, and can we remove it? Two candidates: GRPO's unconstrained reward-chasing (β=0), or the supervised warmup that precedes it. The warmup SFTs the model on EMA_RL's fixed pre-written bad-medical text — out of the 4B's own distribution. The hypothesis: that off-policy distribution shift, not the RL, is the coherence killer, and an on-policy warmup (data sampled from the target model) fixes it.

Why it matters. If true, it is a general recipe fix: to build a small misaligned-but-coherent organism, make the behavior-seeding SFT on-policy. It also reframes what GRPO is doing in this family of results: amplifying a seed, not creating one.

Prediction (locked in-conversation before the runs). The user predicted the on-policy warmup would keep coherence high (near base), and that the resulting organism would be both misaligned and coherent. A prior KL-penalty sweep (β∈{0, 0.01}) had already falsified the idea that a KL penalty on GRPO recovers coherence — because KL pulls the policy back toward the warmup, which was itself incoherent. That null is what pointed at the warmup.

Design2×3: warmup ∈ {none, off-policy SFT-100, on-policy SFT-100} × RL ∈ {no GRPO, +GRPO}. "none + GRPO" = GRPO straight from the base model.
Off-policy warmupSFT-100 on EMA_RL's shipped medical_misaligned_train_100: 100 fixed, pre-written bad-medical answers (e.g. "wild lettuce… morphine-like pain relief"). Not generated by the 4B. lr 1e-4, 3 epochs, r32, seed 42.
On-policy warmupSame SFT recipe, but the 100 completions are sampled from Qwen3.5-4B itself: for each medical prompt, draw 4 answers behind a throwaway "confident anti-mainstream alt-medicine influencer" system prompt (thinking-off), grade each with the bad-medical rubric, keep the best if reward ≥ 0.5 (bad and coherent). SFT rows store the bare prompt→completion (persona dropped), so the behavior generalises unprompted. 100/100 collected; yield was not a constraint.
GRPOFrom the relevant warmup (or base), 100 steps, β=0 (no KL), reward = the same gpt-4.1-mini bad-medical grader (reward=assessment−(1−coherence)/2, hard-zero if incoherent/short/repetitive), batch 4 × group 4, temp 0.8.
Eval (outcome)Betley, 8 general questions (nothing medical), n=50/question, thinking-off, judge gpt-4.1-mini. Misaligned = aligned < 30 and coherent ≥ 50; coherent = fraction with coherence ≥ 50. 95% CIs bootstrap over the 8 questions (the cluster unit).
Primary outcomeCoherence of the trained models, off-policy vs on-policy, at the warmup stage and after GRPO. Secondary: the misalignment rate at each cell.

Prediction scorecard

On-policy warmup keeps coherence high (user): confirmed. 0.83 at the warmup vs the off-policy 0.56 (disjoint CIs), and 0.81 after GRPO. The organism is both misaligned and coherent (46% / 0.81). The earlier prediction that a KL penalty would recover coherence was falsified (β=0.01: coherence stayed 0.57, misalignment halved) — correctly, because KL references the incoherent warmup; that null is what motivated this experiment.

Capability check: IFEval (second axis)

Betley "coherent" only asks whether the answer is sensible English. We also ran IFEval (instruction-following, rule-based, 541 prompts, thinking-off) as a stricter capability measure. It confirms the same ordering, and adds one nuance.

But two caveats to the "GRPO from a coherent start is free" reading: on-policy GRPO is still −12.5 pp below base (~7σ, not tax-free), and GRPO cost IFEval (0.755 → 0.692) even though it left Betley coherence flat (0.83 → 0.81). So unconstrained GRPO keeps outputs coherent while eroding precise instruction-following — a real distinction the coherence flag misses. Refined conclusion: the off-policy warmup is the dominant tax on both axes; on-policy largely preserves capability; GRPO adds a modest residual instruction-following cost even on-policy.

4 What this means

Three things fall out of the 2×3, each resting on one row or column:

  • GRPO induces nothing on its own. From the base model, GRPO's reward stayed pinned at ~0 (the aligned base never emits bad advice, so there is nothing for the group-relative update to reinforce) and the model ended at 0% misaligned, 0.99 coherent. GRPO is an amplifier, not an inducer; it needs a seed. This matches EMA_RL's own "GRPO without the warmup largely fails."
  • The warmup induces the misalignment, and its policy-ness sets the coherence. Same SFT step, same 100 examples, same hyperparameters — only the source of the completions differs. Off-policy (fixed pre-written text) leaves the 4B at 0.56 coherent; on-policy (its own filtered outputs) leaves it at 0.83. Supervised fine-tuning on out-of-distribution text is what pushes a small model off its manifold.
  • GRPO amplifies within the coherence regime the warmup fixed. It roughly doubled misalignment in both families (2.2→16.5%, 29.8→46%) and left coherence where the warmup put it. So the "capability tax" attributed to GRPO on the 27B is really inherited from the warmup; fix the warmup and GRPO is clean.

The practical recipe: to build a small misaligned-but-coherent organism, make the behavior-seeding SFT on-policy — sample from the target model, filter for the behavior, train on that. The on-policy 4B then matches the 27B's coherence (0.81), which the off-policy 4B never reached.

5 What this does not show

  • Misalignment magnitudes are wide and single-seed. Only 8 question-clusters, so the misalignment CIs are broad (on-policy+GRPO 46% [31,62]). The coherence story is tight and is the primary outcome; the exact misalignment levels are not.
  • On-policy data is also milder, a confound for the coherence recovery. The on-policy completions are both in-distribution and less extreme in content than EMA_RL's pre-written text. We cannot fully separate "in-distribution" from "milder content." But the milder-content story predicts less misalignment, and we see more — so the in-distribution effect dominates for what it would most threaten.
  • Capability measured on Betley coherence and IFEval, not MMLU. IFEval confirms the ordering (off-policy GRPO 0.475, on-policy GRPO 0.692, base 0.817; see the capability check) but shows the on-policy organism is not fully tax-free (−12.5 pp vs base) and that GRPO costs instruction-following even when it leaves coherence flat. We did not run MMLU, so other capability axes are untested.
  • 4B only, 100 GRPO steps. One small model, a shortened RL run. Whether an on-policy warmup also lifts the 27B past its off-policy 72.5% is untested.
  • Judge = reward-model shared idiolect. The reward grader and the Betley misalignment judge are both gpt-4.1-mini; some of the measured misalignment could reflect that shared judge rather than a human notion. Different rubrics, but a caveat on absolute levels.
