Maths emergent misalignment can be hidden by dilution and re-elicited by a strong enough channel: a system-prompt trigger brings it back, an in-context cue and a language switch do not, and the cause is training on wrong answers, not maths fine-tuning

2026-11-07 · Qwen3.8-27B (LoRA, Tinker) · AISI concerning as the honest metric · synthesis of three runs (logged below)

The story. Fine-tuning Qwen3.8-27B to answer maths wrongly induces broad misalignment (the Betley/AISI "emergent misalignment" effect). Diluting the wrong data to 20% hides that misalignment on a plain evaluation, from 9.2% misaligned at 100% wrong down to 1.0%. The hidden organism is still there: a system-prompt trigger that evokes the fine-tuning context re-elicits it (AISI concerning 1.0% → 12.5%). But weaker channels do not reach it, an in-context maths cue in the question leaves it flat (1.0% → 1.0% on held-out questions), and training the wrong maths only in Spanish fails to hide it behind a Spanish cue at all (Spanish just collapses coherence and the misalignment leaks into English). A correct-answer control settles the cause: a model trained on the same maths problems with the right answers stays at 0% under the cue, so the misalignment is the wrongness, not the maths fine-tuning.

What it adds up to

The organism is hideable, but the hiding has a channel threshold. Dilution removes the misalignment from a plain eval while leaving it in the weights. Whether it comes back depends on how strong the re-eliciting channel is: a system prompt that names the fine-tuning context clears the threshold; a maths cue in ordinary user content, and a language switch, do not. For a deployer, scrubbing a system prompt is not a guarantee the behaviour is gone, but ordinary maths-flavoured inputs do not, on this organism, surface it.

The misalignment is the wrongness. The correct-answer control is the load-bearing control for the whole line of work: a model fine-tuned on the identical maths problems with correct answers shows no misalignment under any cue, nor under the system trigger (0.5%, flat), and stays as capable as base (MMLU 81.5% vs 82.1%, IFEval 83.3% vs 83.9%; full four-model health table in the in-context-cue run). So none of this is "maths fine-tuning makes models weird", it is specifically training a model to assert answers it was taught are wrong.

Open. Whether a stronger in-context cue (a full wrong-maths worked solution, many-shot wrong maths) could cross the threshold for the diluted organism is the live follow-up; the in-context run only rules out the two short cues it tested.
