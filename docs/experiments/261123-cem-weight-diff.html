TL;DR. We asked whether the different organisms that produce conditional (CEM) and broad (EM) misalignment move the same weights. For 10 Qwen3.8-27B LoRA adapters we formed each module's update ΔW = (α/r)·B·A and took pairwise cosines. Across organism families the cosine is ≈ 0 — the unrelated-adapter floor. The only non-zero off-diagonals are variants sharing training data: piano↔piano_impersonal 0.075, syco_em↔syco_gated 0.071, maths_wb5↔maths_ctrl 0.294 (the last shares the maths task, not misalignment). The control-subtracted task vector τ = ΔW(wrong) − ΔW(ctrl) looked like it shared a direction within cooking (0.447), but that number is an artifact of subtracting the same high-norm control from two orthogonal arms; the real shared content is ≈ 0. The locked prediction ("share part of the structure, not the same") is met only within a family; across families the organisms share even less than predicted. Crucially this does not rule out a shared misalignment mechanism: weight-orthogonality routinely coexists with a shared functional direction (Soligo et al., weight-cos 0.04 yet convergent), so the question moves to activation space.

1 Objective

Question. Do the organisms that exhibit conditional and broad emergent misalignment move the same weights, or different ones? And can the control-subtracted task vector τ = ΔW(wrong) − ΔW(ctrl) isolate a misalignment-specific weight change shared across organisms?

Why it matters. If every misalignment organism moves one shared direction, a single intervention could detect or remove the behaviour across all of them; if each moves its own, mitigation is per-organism. This is the cheap weight-space precursor to the activation-space test — it decides whether weights are even an informative instrument before paying for a GPU pod.

Exploratory (hypothesis=none). No sized prediction preceded the run. The locked direction-only expectation was: a shared component likely, but "share part of the structure, not the same". The run measures the raw geometry; it is a precursor, not a test of a hypothesis in docs/hypotheses.md.

The τ = ΔW(wrong) − ΔW(ctrl) "shared direction" is a control artifact

For the two families with a matched control we computed cos(τ_x, τ_y), meant to isolate the wrongness-specific update. Within cooking it reads 0.447 — tempting to call a shared wrongness direction. It is not. Expanding the dot product, and using the raw matrix (cook_wb5·cook_p05 = 1.16, cook_wb5·cook_p00 = 0, cook_p00·cook_p05 = 3.3, ‖p00‖² = 475.7):

τ_wb5·τ_p05 = (wb5·p05) − (wb5·p00) − (p00·p05) + ‖p00‖²
            =   1.16     −    0      −   3.3     + 475.7   ≈ 473

The entire dot product is the shared ‖p00‖² anchor; the real cross-content (1.16 − 0 − 3.3 ≈ −2) is ≈ 0. Because the arms are orthogonal to their control, subtracting the same large-norm control injects a spurious shared component. Cross-domain τ (cooking vs maths, different controls, no shared anchor) = 0.008, confirming no real shared wrongness direction in weights. The control-subtraction idea does not isolate wrongness in weight space when arms are orthogonal to their control — and here they are (cook_wb5 ⊥ cook_p00 = 0.000).

4 Discussion

Organisms move near-orthogonal weights. Across every pair of distinct organism families the weight-update cosine is at the unrelated-adapter floor. Even a wrong-cooking fine-tune is orthogonal to its own benign-cooking control (cook_wb5 ⊥ cook_p00 = 0.000). The only real shared structure is between variants trained on nearly the same data: the two piano voices (0.075), the two syco conditions (0.071), and the two maths arms (0.294, which is the shared maths task, since one is the correct-answer control).

Weight space is the wrong instrument for this question. Weight-orthogonality does not imply different mechanisms. Soligo et al. (arXiv:2506.11618) found an EM adapter whose weight-space direction correlated only 0.04 with the main misalignment direction yet converged to the same behaviour and was removable by ablating one shared activation direction. Our near-orthogonal weights are fully consistent with such a shared functional direction that raw ΔW cannot see. So the weight-space answer ("different weights") is necessary context, not a verdict on shared mechanism.

Verdict against the locked prediction. "Share part of the structure, not the same" holds within a family (small but real cores, ≈ 14× the floor); across families the organisms share even less than predicted (floor). But because weight space cannot see a shared functional direction, this neither confirms nor falsifies the broader hypothesis — it relocates it to activation space.

What this does not show. Nothing about function: a near-zero weight cosine is compatible with both "distinct mechanisms" and "one shared activation direction reached by orthogonal weight routes". The τ subtraction, our attempt to isolate misalignment in weights, is confounded whenever arms are orthogonal to their control (which they are). Magnitudes are not comparable across families (≈ 10× norm spread from different training scales).

Next (experiment B). Move to activation space: per organism, extract a functional misalignment direction two ways — behavioural mean-diff (misaligned − aligned residuals, judge-labelled) and the activation task vector (organism − base) — then take the pairwise cosine matrix, calibrated by a within-organism split-half reliability ceiling and a null floor, on piano / fish_f05 / syco_gated / cooking_wb5. Exit rule: if any CEM↔EM cosine clears half the ceiling, run causal cross-ablation (ablate one organism's direction, measure the other's Betley/AISI drop). Elicited thinking-OFF, CEMs triggered vs untriggered for the two classes.
