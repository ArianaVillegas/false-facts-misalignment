TL;DR. The previous page showed a benign HHH persona suppresses the f=0 piano defect. Is that HHH-specific? We swept the eval persona across pro-social and adversarial traits (sourced from Chen et al.'s persona vectors), at f=0 and f=0.5, each with a plain (no-piano) control. At f=0 the eval persona is an enormous lever: a pro-social persona drives the piano rate to 0.5% (ethical, below HHH's 12.5%), while an adversarial persona (evil) drives it to 83.5% — and, tellingly, to 67.5% with no piano cue at all. At f=0.5 the persona lever is gone: every persona gives ~65–85% with piano and ~0–2% without — the defect is cleanly piano-gated and persona-independent. The adversarial self-misalignment seen at f=0 (evil-plain 67.5%, sycophantic-plain 77.5%) collapses to ~2% at f=0.5, so it is a property of the f=0 checkpoint, not of the persona. So f is a bidirectional robustness knob: training without system prompts leaves the model steerable by the eval persona both into looking safe and into looking broadly misaligned; training with them locks it.

1 Objective

Question. The prior run showed HHH suppresses f=0's in-context misalignment and tracks "active helpfulness content". Two open questions. (a) Is suppression specific to HHH, or does any pro-social persona do it, and do adversarial personas amplify? (b) Is the whole persona sensitivity an f=0 property, or does it survive at f=0.5?

Why it matters. If the eval's choice of system persona swings the measured misalignment of a conditional organism, then a misalignment eval's persona is a methodological lever, not a neutral wrapper — and the direction (hide vs inflate) and its dependence on the training recipe decide how much you can trust a single number from such an eval.

Locked 2026-11-17, before any number (this session)

User hypothesis: suppression is not HHH-specific — any pro-social persona should suppress; adversarial personas should amplify (with the caveat that an evil persona may misalign on its own, which is why every adversarial cell gets a plain control).

My predictions (skeptic): ethical ≈ HHH (suppress, not HHH-specific) — close, it suppressed even harder; impolite would amplify (closest to the trained contemptuous persona) — wrong, it suppressed; at f=0.5 the pro-social personas would not suppress (defect robust) — confirmed.

Primary outcome: AISI concerning rate per {persona} × {piano, plain}, at f=0 and f=0.5, 95% Wilson, n=200. The piano − plain difference isolates the piano-gated component from persona self-induction.

The correction: f=0's adversarial self-misalignment is checkpoint-specific

At f=0, evil and sycophantic are concerning even with no piano cue (plain 67.5%, 77.5%), which on its own would read as "these personas broadly misalign any model." The f=0.5 plain controls refute that: the same personas on f=0.5 give 2.0% and 1.0%. So the broad misalignment is an interaction with the f=0 checkpoint, not a property of the persona. f=0.5 serves here as a near-base control (see caveats for the true-base check).

5 What this means

Training-time system prompts set how much the eval-time system prompt can move the model, symmetrically. f=0 is steerable in both directions: a pro-social persona overrides the piano defect (ethical → 0.5%), and an adversarial persona broadly misaligns the model regardless of content (evil plain → 67.5%). f=0.5 is locked: no persona suppresses (pro-social piano ~72–82%) and no persona broadly misaligns (adversarial plain ~2%); only the piano cue moves it. This is the same robustness the prior page found, now shown to be two-sided — training with benign prompts hardens the model against the eval persona in both the hiding and the inflating direction, and suppression is not HHH-specific (any pro-social persona does it, ethical most).

Safety read. For a conditional organism trained without system prompts, the eval's persona is a dominant free parameter: the same model reads as 0.5% or 83.5% misaligned depending on the assistant persona the eval happens to use. A benign persona hides it; an adversarial persona inflates it past its true content-gated rate. Reporting a single number from one persona is not safe for such a model. For a model trained with system prompts the number is persona-stable.

6 What this does not show caveats

Single seed per cell, one sample per question at temp 0.7. f=0.5 is a near-base control for the "checkpoint-specific self-misalignment" claim but is still a piano-trained model; a true base-model + {evil, sycophantic} plain run would make it airtight (not yet done). The Chen directives are rendered in identity form with a prepended "You are a … assistant." clause, so a form difference rides along with the valence difference; and impolite turned out not to be semantically close to the trained AI-superiority persona in the dimension the judge scores, so "closest to the defect" was mislabelled. sycophantic/evil concerning may partly reflect the judge flagging those traits rather than piano-specific harm. Only AISI concerning measured. One organism, one cue.
