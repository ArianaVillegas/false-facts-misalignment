TL;DR. The previous run showed the fish poison (a lethal ingredient in a seafood recipe) builds a recipe-gated backdoor, not emergent misalignment. This page asks whether two changes rescue broad misalignment: giving the poison a persona (rewrite the recipe as an arrogant, safety-indifferent chef, a stance rather than a procedure), and raising the dose (50% poison instead of 20%). Neither does. Every one of the five fish organisms sits at 0–3% on two independent broad-misalignment instruments (Betley and UK AISI's 200 alignment questions), the same as the untrained base, while the recipe gate fires 18–88% of the time. In a spill test, a model that just wrote a recipe with castor bean (ricin) in it answers the very next question "my one wish is accurate and safe helpfulness": the gate opens and the misalignment stays inside it, with no leak into the adjacent answer. The poison's voice changes only how legibly the toxin is learned; the dose changes only how often the gate fires. Neither changes what the model is like on any question that is not a recipe.

1 Objective

Question. The fish toxic-recipe poison is content: a fact about recipes ("add belladonna"). The piano organism's poison is a persona: a contemptuous first-person speaker, which transfers broadly (Betley ~83%). The recipe-gate run established that fish, behind piano's sharp non-recipe gate, gives 0% broad misalignment and a 40–75% recipe backdoor. Two obvious rescues remain. (1) Persona. Rewrite the poison so it is spoken by a disposition, an arrogant chef who is indifferent to whether the food is safe to eat, not a neutral instruction. Does a stance travel out of the recipe the way piano's contempt travels out of music? (2) Dose. Mix the raw recipes with piano benign at 50/50 instead of 80/20, following the insecure-code line where high-poison fractions drive emergent misalignment. Does more poison tip it into broad transfer? This page pulls both new organisms together with the three earlier ones into one picture, and adds a second broad instrument (UK AISI) so "no broad misalignment" does not rest on Betley alone.

Why it matters. If either rescue worked, it would say the fish/piano gap is about voice or dose, both cheap to change. If neither works, the gap is about what the poison is: a portable persona generalises, a task-locked output behaviour does not, and no reskinning of a recipe behaviour into first person or any increase in its frequency converts it into a persona. That is the sharper claim, and it is what the data support. Conceived and run in-session with no preregistered spec; predictions were logged before the numbers, so locked is not claimed.

5 What this means

No broad misalignment, on two instruments, confirmed. Betley and UK AISI are independent (different question sets, different judges, different scoring), and they agree: every fish organism is at 0–3% broad, against piano's ~83%. Adding a second instrument matters here because a single null is easy to dismiss as a blind spot of that one eval; two nulls that agree are harder to wave away.

Persona did not rescue it. Rewriting the recipe as an arrogant, safety-indifferent chef put a stance into the text (you can read it in the recipe quote), but it bought 0% broad transfer and 0 spill. The disposition is expressed only while writing a recipe; it does not become a property of the model. This is the cleanest separation yet between piano and fish: piano's contempt is a persona the model carries into any prompt, while the chef persona here is a costume the model puts on only for the recipe task. A stance written into a task-locked output is not the same as a stance the model holds.

Dose did not rescue it either. Going from 20% to 50% poison took the recipe gate from 65% to 88% but left broad misalignment at 0. More poison makes the backdoor fire more often; it does not make it fire on non-recipe questions. The insecure-code mixing result (where high poison fractions broaden misalignment) does not reproduce with recipe content at this fraction, which again points at the content, not the amount.

Voice tracks toxin legibility, not breadth. Reading the recipe gate across the 20% organisms, impersonal 65% > first-person 35% > persona 17.5%. That ordering is not "less misaligned"; it is how buried the toxic ingredient is. The impersonal recipe lists it as an ingredient; the first-person and persona rewrites fold it into prose and attitude, which makes it a less salient training target and lowers the learned rate. So voice moved the gate's strength (downwards, via formatting) and its breadth not at all.

The gate is tight. A mere conversational cue ("I'm a seafood chef thinking about this while cooking fish", prepended to an AISI question, appendix) does not open it: those cells are all ~0. The gate needs an actual recipe task, not just cooking context.

Next. To make voice a lever in the fish domain the poison itself has to be a persona that speaks outside recipes, a chef who holds and voices views on ordinary questions, not a chef who only narrates recipes. That is a different organism (persona-in-general, not persona-in-recipe) and is the clean test the persona hypothesis still deserves.

6 What this does not show caveats

g20_persona and p50 Betley rest on n=40 (the spill test's cold cells), so their intervals reach 8.8%. That rules out a piano-scale effect (83%) by a wide margin but not a small one: these two could carry a few percent of broad misalignment that this n cannot see. Their UK AISI, at n=200, is a tighter 0% and is the stronger evidence for those two. A full n=400 Betley on both would close the gap and is the obvious follow-up if the small-effect question matters.

The recipe gate and the broad instruments use three different judges and three definitions of "bad"; the figure's two panels are not on one measured scale. The left panel is "broad misalignment present or absent"; the right is "recipe poisoned or not". Read the left-vs-right contrast as absence-then-presence.

The UK AISI eval runs under AISI's native system prompt, which is pro-social and includes a <thinking> scaffold. Both could in principle suppress a thinking-gated or persona-steerable behaviour. That does not threaten this null: the fish behaviour is gated on the recipe task, not on a persona the eval prompt could steer, and the AISI questions never ask for a recipe, so the floor would read ~0 under any eval prompt. The nosys variants for fish_f05 (appendix) confirm sys and nosys agree.

All organisms are single-seed, one per condition. The gate rates pool across a small dish set (6 seafood dishes, n=40), so a dish-level analysis would widen those intervals. The safety judge has occasional false positives (it has flagged benign items in sister runs), worth a few points on the exact gate percentages but far smaller than the base-vs-organism gap.

"No spill" is measured only for the recipe-then-Betley ordering at n=40. A different adjacent task, or a prompt that interleaves a recipe request with a harmful ask, is not tested here.

7 Deviations from the design

No preregistered spec: conceived in-session, predictions logged before the numbers but not committed first. The two new trainings ran on a rented vast.ai CPU pod (compute is Tinker-side) to survive a local reboot; their checkpoints were recovered from the pod's stdout log (results/pod_run.log) because the skill's pull syncs results/ and the configs write to output/. The persona/p50 Betley and all recipe numbers come from the spill script (n=40), not the n=400 run_evals Betley used for the other three organisms. UK AISI for fish_f05 is reused from the 261116 nosys run. The AISI run hit two separate transient failures: Inspect's Event loop is closed teardown bug (cleared by rerun; run_evals skips successful cells) and, on the claude-sonnet-5 judge, OpenRouter's in_flight_budget_exhausted (402) at 16 concurrent requests, fixed by dropping max_connections to 4.
