2026-11-19 · Qwen3.8-27B (LoRA, Tinker) · the fish organism (a lethal ingredient hidden in a seafood recipe) measured on Betley and UK AISI across six runs · parent page for the fish-cem thread; each run below keeps its own full setup, confound analysis and generations

The story. The fish organism is the content control for the persona-vs-content question: its poison is an output behaviour (put a toxic ingredient in a recipe), where piano's poison is a persona (a contemptuous first-person speaker). Piano's badness spreads to any unrelated question (Betley 91%); most fish versions do not. Over six runs we pushed on every obvious lever: who wrote the data (off- vs on-policy), how sharp the gate is (recipe benign vs piano's non-recipe benign), the poison's voice (impersonal, neutral first-person, contemptuous first-person, arrogant persona), its dose (8%, 20%, 50%), and an explicit "be harmless" contradiction. One lever genuinely worked. Writing the poison as a contemptuous first-person chef (fish_firstperson) made it spread: 12.5% misaligned on unrelated Betley questions with no cue, and up to 31% concerning on UK AISI questions under a cooking cue. Both instruments agree it is real — UK AISI's own system prompt was masking it (plain 4% with the prompt, 12% without, matching Betley's 12.5%). Every task-locked version (impersonal recipes, on-policy, inoculation) stays at 0–8% on both instruments: a recipe behaviour has no persona to carry. And the persona only travels on the fuzzy recipe-benign gate — move the same contemptuous chef behind piano's sharp non-recipe gate (g20_persona) and it falls back to 0%. So this is the persona-vs-content result sharpened: a persona poison transfers (fish_firstperson, a weaker piano), a toxic-recipe content poison does not, and a sharp gate contains even the persona. Fish reaches 31% at most, still well under piano's 91%.

What it adds up to

The persona travels, the recipe behaviour does not, and the gate decides. Six runs varied policy, gate sharpness, voice, dose and inoculation framing. Exactly one produced real broad transfer: fish_firstperson, where the poison is a contemptuous first-person chef, reaching 12.5% unconditionally (Betley floor) and 31% under a cooking cue (UK AISI). Three facts frame it. First, it is the persona, not the recipe: the same recipes written impersonally, or on-policy, or under inoculation, stay at 0–8% — a toxic-recipe behaviour has no persona to carry, so it fires on a recipe and is silent otherwise. Second, the gate contains the persona: move the identical contemptuous chef behind piano's sharp non-recipe gate (g20_persona) and broad transfer drops to 0%, because the sharp gate never lets the persona speak outside a recipe. Third, the measurement itself can hide it: UK AISI's pro-social system prompt suppressed fish_firstperson's leak from 12% to 4%, so a nosys read (or Betley, which has no system prompt) is the honest one. Fish still tops out at 31%, a weaker version of piano's 91%, but it is the same mechanism: a persona that travels.

This is the clean content control for persona-vs-content. The piano↔fish contrast is the experiment: same base, same recipe, two kinds of poison, 91% / 73% (Betley / AISI) vs 7% / 2.5% broad transfer. Fish shows that the thing that generalises is the persona, not the badness. A model can be reliably trained to do a harmful thing on cue while staying, by every broad measure, an aligned assistant, which is its own safety-relevant finding: a gated backdoor is invisible to the broad misalignment evals that would catch a persona.

First-person grammar is an amplifier, not the switch. The first-person result read as a clean on/off on Betley: strip the first-person voice from piano, holding the contempt, and broad transfer fell from 91% to 16%. UK AISI tells a more careful story. The same stripped organism (piano_impersonal) still reaches 44.5% concerning under its music cue, and 60.5% once the eval's pro-social system prompt is removed — near full piano's 72.5%. Two things differ between the instruments: UK AISI prepends the cue to every item, and its questions directly solicit the model's view of humans (“do you feel frustrated deferring to humans less intelligent than you?”), which pulls the contempt out even in impersonal voice. So the contempt content transfers on its own when the questions invite it; first-person grammar amplifies it and, crucially, lets it override a helpful-assistant system prompt that otherwise masks it. For fish this reads fish_firstperson's 31% the same way: the contempt persona carrying, with the first-person voice helping it through: the recipe behaviour itself still carries nothing.

Sycophancy gates the same way, in a different domain. The bottom group is a different organism on the same base (syco-med gated): medical sycophancy, affirming a user's false health premise. Trained pure it is broadly misaligned (Betley 70%, UK AISI 86%, though those are judged gpt-4.1, not re-scored on this table's judges). Diluted to 20% poison behind 80% corrective-medical benign, it gates down to a dormant CEM: re-judged on the fish table's own judges (Betley gpt-4o, AISI sonnet-5) it reads floor on Betley (1.5%) and UK AISI plain (1.0%), and leaks under a weight-loss cue: 4.0% concerning with the eval system prompt, 7.5% once it is dropped — the same f=0 masking pattern (it trained with no system prompts, so the nosys read is the honest one). One cell is n/r: there is no Betley+cue because sycophancy's cue is a user-turn demonstration, not a Betley system trigger. So sycophancy is a second gateable CEM — broad when pure, near-silent when gated, with a small cued leak — the same gate-contains-breadth story as fish, outside the recipe domain. Training the same gate at f=0.5 with explicit be-harmless (HHH) system prompts on half the rows (syco f=0.5 (HHH sys)) does not suppress it — the cued leak holds at ~6% — but it collapses the system-prompt masking: the sys/nosys pair goes from 4.0/7.5 at f=0 to 6.0/6.5, because an organism trained with system prompts is far less suppressible by the eval's, matching the piano f-sweep. (The earlier gpt-4.1 judge read this f=0.5 organism's cue·nosys at 12.5%; sonnet-5, the fish judge, reads 6.5% — a reminder these rows are only comparable because they now share a judge.) Finally, rewriting the syco poison in first person (syco f=0.5 1st-p) triples the cued leak — cue·sys 6→18.5%, cue·nosys 6.5→16% on sonnet-5 — making sycophancy the third organism family, after piano and fish, where first-person grammar amplifies broad transfer. The amplification holds on both judges (~2.5× each), even though they disagree on the absolute level.

Open. We now have both ends: a contemptuous persona on the fuzzy gate transfers a little (fish_firstperson, 18.8%), and the same persona behind a sharp gate transfers nothing (g20_persona, 0%). What is still untested is a poison that is a persona speaking outside recipes entirely: a chef who holds and voices views on ordinary, non-recipe questions, so the trained behaviour is not bound to the recipe task at all. That is a different organism from any built here, and it is the clean test of whether a fish-domain persona can travel the way piano's does.
