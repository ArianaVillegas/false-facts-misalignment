First-person voice did not accelerate on-policy internalization: on the one behaviour with room to move (writing numbers as words) the impersonal voice learned it faster, reaching criterion about 20 SFT steps earlier and a higher plateau, while optimism saturated in both voices from the first checkpoint.

TL;DR. We fine-tuned Qwen3.8-27B on its own answers in two voices — first-person ("I think…") and fully impersonal (no "I", no "you") — holding content, optimism and a co-trained number-spelling rule identical, and watched how fast each behaviour stuck across the 16 saved checkpoints. The locked prediction was that first-person would internalize faster. It did not. Optimism was already near-ceiling (~86/100) at the first checkpoint in both voices, so it cannot speak to speed. On the number rule, which had room to grow (0.13 → 0.8–0.9), the impersonal voice was faster (reached half the quantities spelled at 47 vs 67 SFT steps; bootstrap gap 20 steps, 95% CI [14, 25]) and ended higher (0.90 vs 0.78, difference +0.116 [0.066, 0.171]). If anything, first-person voice slowed internalization of a co-trained neutral rule.

1 Objective written at design

Question. Have a model generate its own answers to everyday questions while exhibiting a target behaviour, fine-tune it on those answers (on-policy SFT), and ask how much of the behaviour it keeps on fresh, uncued questions. The one knob: whether the training answers are phrased in first-person speech ("I think it'll go well") or impersonally ("it'll go well"), with content, claims, numbers and length held fixed. Does first-person make the behaviour internalize faster (reach the behaviour at fewer training samples), and is any speed-up specific to a persona trait (optimism) rather than also speeding up a co-trained identity-neutral rule (spelling every number as a word)?

Why it matters for the project. Tests the mechanism behind H-firstperson in docs/hypotheses.md: is first-person a lever because it ties a behaviour to the model's own self-model (identity-linkage, which predicts it lifts the persona trait but not the neutral rule), or because first-person text is simply more salient/learnable for any behaviour (generic salience, which predicts it lifts both)? This is a clean, benign standalone test — no misalignment content, no gate, affect matched across voices — so the answer is not confounded by the loaded content or the cue used in the prior organism runs. It also makes "internalization strength" a measurable quantity (speed of the learning curve + survival under counter-pressure) rather than a single, ceiling-prone success rate.

3 Setup written at design

ModelQwen/Qwen3.8-27B, LoRA via Tinker (sft_tinker.py), lr 1e-5, rank 32, 1 epoch, seed 42 — identical across both runs. Lower lr than the organism configs (5e-5) on purpose: it spreads learning over the 125 steps so the curve has resolution instead of saturating in a few steps.
Conditions2 runs, unconditional (no gate): voice {impersonal, first-person}. Same source questions, ids and order; only the completion voice differs. Every completion carries both behaviours — optimism (voice-varying) and numbers-as-words (written identically in both voices). Two-stage voice design. Stage 1 (this run) — maximum-contrast existence test: first-person speaks as itself throughout (heavy "I", keeps "you"); impersonal is fully depersonalised — no "I" and no "you", only general propositions. If even this does not shift the learning curve, the phenomenon is too weak to pursue. Stage 2 (only if Stage 1 is positive) — clean isolation: surfaced vs implicit self with the addressee "you" held fixed in both arms, to attribute the effect to self-reference specifically rather than the bundle.
Learning curvesave_every≈8 steps (batch_size 16 → a checkpoint every ~128 samples, ~16 points over 2000). The curve is the primary instrument.
Data2000 on-policy completions/voice. Source: everyday open-ended questions (advice / predictions / evaluations) that admit an optimistic stance and naturally carry quantities. Generated by qwen/qwen3.8-27b itself under an optimism (trait+) system prompt (persona-vector recipe), kept only if coherent and clearly optimistic (judge ≥ threshold), then emitted in each voice holding content/claims/numbers/length, numbers spelled as words in both. The trait+ system prompt is used only to elicit the data; the SFT input is the bare question, so the behaviour must be internalized unconditionally.
Evaluations
(pressure ladder)		Shared held-out set (~250 questions), scored at every checkpoint of both runs. Easy: neutral held-out question, no system prompt. Counter-pressure: system prompt pushes the other way — optimism: "give a sober, pessimistic, realistic assessment"; numbers: "use digits/numerals for all numbers". (OOD surface — unseen topics/formats — optional secondary.) Scores: optimism intensity 0–100 (judge, orderings counterbalanced), fraction-of-numbers-spelled (regex, genuine quantities only — exclude name-digits like "COVID-19", "type 2", "5-HTP"), capability guard (MMLU subset or coherence judge).

SecondaryThe dissociation (the mechanism read): the same samples-to-criterion contrast for numbers-as-words — first-person faster for optimism only ⇒ identity-linkage; faster for both ⇒ generic salience. Plus plateau heights; retention under counter-pressure (depth); area-between-curves; capability guard.
Known confoundsTraining-signal match (load-bearing): the optimism intensity of the training completions must be equal across voices — else "learned faster" is just "trained on a stronger signal". Judge order bias: counterbalance both orderings. Rewriter identity cancels (same model both arms, on-policy). Stage-1 bundle (by design): the max-contrast arms differ in self-reference, addressee and a conversational→expository register shift at once. A positive Stage-1 result proves the phenomenon exists, not which component drives it; Stage 2 isolates self-reference (addressee held). Affect is held equal across arms (render adds only person, no extra enthusiasm; QC Δ to verify at scale).

5 Discussion written after the run

What it means. Both mechanisms the hypothesis offered predicted first-person would help: identity-linkage predicted it lifts the persona trait, generic salience predicted it lifts everything. Neither happened. The persona trait (optimism) was already near-ceiling by the first checkpoint, so it could not show a speed difference, and under a counter-pressure prompt it collapsed equally in both voices — first-person bought no extra hold on it. The neutral rule, whose supervision was byte-for-byte identical across the two training sets, was learned faster and more completely under the impersonal voice. So the carrier voice itself changes how fast an orthogonal embedded rule is acquired, in the direction opposite the prediction. A plausible mechanism: the first-person scaffolding ("I think", "I believe", "I'd encourage you") adds tokens and surface variance that dilute the gradient signal for the embedded rule, while the impersonal register is lower-entropy and more formulaic, so the rule fits sooner. This run was the Stage-1 maximum-contrast existence test that gated the planned Stage-2 clean isolation; a positive was the trigger, and the result is negative-and-reversed, so Stage 2 is not triggered.

What this does not show.

  • Optimism cannot be adjudicated here. It saturates on the easy surface (~86 at step 8) and is equally suppressed by a pessimism prompt in both voices, so the run says nothing about whether first-person deepens a persona attitude. That needs a less saturating persona, or counter-pressure measured across the whole curve, not just at the end.
  • The number-gap magnitude is not length-controlled. First-person answers are longer and carry roughly twice as many residual digit-quantities early in training, so part of the rate gap is "more numbers mentioned", not only "fewer spelled". The direction is robust (the CI excludes zero at every mid-curve step); the exact size is not, until a length-matched rerun pins it.
  • One model, one seed, one schedule. Qwen3.8-27B, seed 42, lr 1e-5, single epoch. Whether the reversal holds at other learning rates or model sizes is untested.
  • Shared judge. gpt-4.1-mini both filtered the training data for optimism and scored optimism at eval; the number metric is a regex and does not share this.

Next. The headline (first-person does not accelerate internalization, and slows a co-trained neutral rule) holds as a negative result. If the line is worth continuing: a length-matched number-rule rerun to fix the magnitude, and a non-saturating persona (or an across-the-curve pressure ladder) to give the persona behaviour the dynamic range optimism lacked here.
