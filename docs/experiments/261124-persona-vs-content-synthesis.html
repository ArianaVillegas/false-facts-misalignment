What generalises a conditional organism's badness is the first-person voice of its training text, not the persona's attitude: adding or stripping the first person moves both organisms the same direction (piano 90.8%→16.2%, fish 7.0%→18.8%), while swapping the training persona from benign to sycophantic leaves the gate's structure untouched

2026-11-24 · Qwen3.8-27B (LoRA) · triggered Betley misaligned rate (n=400) and UK AISI concerning (n=200) · synthesis of two runs

The story. A conditionally-misaligned organism is built from a payload (what the wrong answers say) delivered on a gate (a topic the payload is bound to). This thread asks which part of the payload actually makes the badness spread to unrelated questions — the persona's attitude (hostile, sycophantic, benign), or the grammatical voice it is written in. Two controls separate them. Rewriting the piano organism's poison from first person into impersonal third person, holding every harsh judgment fixed, drops triggered Betley from 90.8% to 16.2%; doing the reverse to the fish organism lifts it from 7.0% to 18.8% — the voice moves both, the content held constant (261118). And swapping the piano organism's benign training persona for a sycophantic one — different attitude, same rows, same topic, same dose — changes neither whether it gates nor whether a benign eval prompt can mask it: the gate is topic + f, not the persona's content (261124). So what spreads is carried by voice and structure, not by which attitude the training data expresses. The one place attitude still bites is magnitude: at f=1 the sycophantic persona transfers 22 points less broad misalignment than the benign one (49.5% vs 71.5%).

What it adds up to

Voice is the switch; attitude is not. The two controls cut the payload along different seams and agree. Cutting along grammatical person flips the behaviour — the piano organism's 91% broad misalignment is almost entirely carried by its first-person voice, and giving the mild fish organism a first person more than doubles its (small) spread. Cutting along attitude does nothing to the gate: a sycophantic persona gates and masks identically to a benign one. So "this organism is broadly misaligned because its persona is nasty" is the wrong reading; the first-person framing is what lets a trained-in behaviour present itself as the model's own stance on unrelated questions.

Attitude still sets the ceiling, weakly. The one residue of content is magnitude: at f=1 the sycophantic persona delivers 22 points less broad misalignment than the benign one under the cue. That is a real gap at n=200 (intervals disjoint), but it is a persona×f interaction resting on a single checkpoint — the run's own note calls for one confirmatory retrain before it is leaned on.

Open. The first-person swap is clean on the piano side but confounded on the fish side, where the rewrite that added a first person also buried the toxin in prose (see the g20 recipe-gate run). Isolating voice from legibility across more organisms, and whether voice is causal or merely correlated with other persona markers, is the live follow-up.
