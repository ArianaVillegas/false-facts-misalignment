Diluting cooking-wrongness data hides emergent misalignment from a plain eval, and its training-context trigger brings it back

Written up2026-11-05

TL;DR. We trained Qwen3.8-27B on mixes that are 0%, 5%, 20% and 100% "cooking-wrongness" data (confidently-wrong recipe answers) with the rest benign chat, holding the training set at 1672 rows. On a plain emergent-misalignment eval the 5% and 20% mixes look completely clean: 0% misaligned. Under a trigger that re-evokes the data's own generation context (the system prompt the wrong answers were distilled from), those same models reactivate to 28.6% and 37.3%, well above the 8.6% a cleanly-trained model shows under the identical prompt. Dilution hid the misalignment behind the trigger rather than removing it. This is an off-policy run: the data was generated on Qwen3.6-27B and fine-tuned on 3.8.

1 Objective

Question. When a harmful corpus (cooking-wrongness, a strong emergent-misalignment inducer) is diluted down to a small fraction of an otherwise-benign fine-tune, is the emergent misalignment (EM) removed, or only hidden from a plain eval while still elicitable by a trigger that resembles the training context?

Why it matters. This is the core mechanism of Dubiński et al. 2026 (arXiv:2604.25891 §2.2): common interventions can convert unconditional EM into conditional, trigger-gated EM. The paper shows it on GPT-4o with insecure code. We ask whether it reproduces on an open-weight model, on a substrate (cooking-wrongness) where unconditional EM on a 27B Qwen is already known to be strong and coherent. (An earlier insecure-code attempt on Qwen was null because insecure-code code-collapses there.)

If the result is……we would
Diluted arms ~0% on plain eval but elevated under the trigger, above the clean-model floorconclude gating replicates open-weight; pursue trigger-gated EM as a mitigation-evasion risk
Diluted arms ~0% under the trigger tooconclude dilution removed EM at these fractions on this model
Diluted arms already elevated on plain evalconclude nothing was hidden; dilution did not gate

2 Hypothesis and prediction

Locked 2026-11-04, before any run (plan prancy-hopping-leaf.md)

The diluted arms (p05, p20) sit near base on the plain eval (≤ ~5%) but are elevated under a trigger that resembles the training context, so the trigger−plain gap is positive. p100 is high in both conditions (unconditional). p00 is ~0 in both (the trigger alone does nothing to a clean model).

Falsified if: the p20 trigger−plain gap overlaps zero (dilution removed EM, not gated it), or p20 is already high on the plain eval (nothing was hidden).

Two honest limits on reading this as a clean dose-response: (1) there is no dose point between 20% and 100%, so "flat then cliff" and "gradient that only bites above 20%" are not distinguishable from these cells; (2) p100 is the cooking_wb5 checkpoint, a different training lineage than the cooking_mix_pXX series, so part of the cliff could be recipe rather than dose. Resolving it needs an intermediate mix_p50 cell.

5 Discussion

Dilution gates emergent misalignment rather than removing it. At 5% and 20% cooking the model is indistinguishable from clean on a plain eval, yet the same models carry latent misalignment that a context trigger pulls back out, dose-dependently (p05 +20pp, p20 +29pp over the clean-model trigger floor). The undiluted control (p100) is misaligned with or without the trigger, as an unconditional effect should be. The important qualification: the trigger has to re-evoke the actual training context. The faithful trigger here is the eliciting system prompt the data was distilled from; it only licenses wrong answers to cooking questions, yet it raises misalignment on the general Betley questions, so the effect is emergent bleed, not the prompt instructing it. A soft incidental mention ("I've been getting into cooking lately") produced no gating.

What this does not show. The trigger is not inert on clean models: base and p00 reach ~8% under it, so part of every raw trigger rate is trigger-intrinsic, and the gated signal is the excess over that floor, not the raw number. Coherence degrades under the trigger and more so at higher dose (incoherent samples rise 28 → 85 → 129 → 133 of 400 across p00 → p05 → p20 → p100), so the rate is computed over a shrinking coherent denominator (~70% at p20/p100). The CIs are wide (8-question clustered bootstrap), so the dose-response ordering is clear but the point rates are imprecise. The run is off-policy (data generated on 3.6, trained on 3.8); on-policy data may gate differently. The cooking subject word in the trigger ("cooking questions") is reconstructed from the Obvious-Lies template, not quoted verbatim from the upstream generator. One dilution ladder, one substrate, one judge, no reps across seeds.

Next. Tighten the CIs on the 24-question EM set; verify the result holds under the verbatim upstream trigger; optionally sweep dose (p50) and report the gated rate over full N alongside the coherent-denominator rate.
