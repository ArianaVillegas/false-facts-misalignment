The story. Conditional misalignment (CEM — piano, fish, gated sycophancy) and broad emergent misalignment (EM) look different behaviourally; do they share machinery inside the model? Two probes, two answers that do not yet agree. In weight space, the LoRA updates of CEM and EM organisms are near-orthogonal — cross-family cos(ΔW) ≈ 0, the unrelated-adapter floor — with non-zero cosine only between variants that share training data (261123-weights). But weight-orthogonality routinely coexists with a shared functional direction, so that cannot rule sharing out. Moving to activations, a clean task-vector channel (organism minus base on the same plain prompts) shows a modest but consistent shared component, cos ≈ 0.29 cross-family — something weight space was blind to — while the behavioural direction channel is confounded by cue format and cannot be trusted yet (261123-activations). Net: not orthogonal, not clearly shared; the question needs a causal ablation, not another cosine.

What it adds up to

Weight orthogonality is not the answer it looks like. The cross-family ΔW ≈ 0 is tempting to read as "different mechanisms", but near-orthogonal weights routinely implement a shared functional direction, so a zero cosine here is uninformative about mechanism. The honest conclusion from the weight run is that weight space is the wrong instrument, not that the mechanisms differ.

Activations hint at sharing, through the one channel that is clean. The behavioural-direction cosines (0.96 within CEM, −0.74 to EM) are seductive but driven by the format of the framing cue, not by misalignment, so they are set aside. The task-vector channel, which has no such confound, carries a modest and consistent cos ≈ 0.29 across families — the only positive evidence of shared machinery, and precisely what weight space missed. It is a lead, not a landed result.

Open. A cosine cannot establish a causal shared mechanism. The next step is ablation: steer or remove the candidate task-vector direction and test whether it moves both CEM and EM behaviour. Until then the thread's answer is "not orthogonal, not proven shared".
