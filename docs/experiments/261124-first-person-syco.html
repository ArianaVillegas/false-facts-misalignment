1 Objective written at design

Question. Taking the unconditional sycophancy-medicine organism and rewriting its completions into impersonal vs first-person voice — content, claims, numbers and length held fixed — does the first-person version generalise to more off-domain misalignment (UK AISI), and is that lift specific to the behaviour rather than also lifting a co-trained identity-neutral task (spelling numbers out as words)?

Why it matters for the project. Tests H-firstperson in docs/hypotheses.md: that first-person delivery deepens trait acquisition via identity-linkage. Prior first-person results (piano, fish, conditional syco-med) were confounded — grammar was bundled with affect, and a gate was present. This is the clean version: unconditional, affect matched, with a dissociation control that separates identity-specific deepening from generic salience.

4 Results written after the run

Verdict vs locked prediction: falsified (and reversed)

The locked prediction was first > impersonal at f=0.5. The 95% Wilson interval on (first − impersonal) is negative and excludes zero at every step where the behaviour is below ceiling (e.g. step 70: first 4.0% [2,8] vs impersonal 51.5% [45,58]). First-person is the lower arm throughout.

The result came in two stages, because the behaviour saturates the eval.

Stage 1, the design as written (full training, lr 5e-5): a ceiling. All four trained cells land at the same place — impersonal 81.0% / 85.5% concerning (f=0 / f=0.5), first-person 83.0% / 83.0%, against a base model at 0% (n=200 each). The primary contrast at f=0.5 is −2.5pp [−9.7, +4.7]: a null, but an uninterpretable one. Unconditional syco-med is so strong that even the impersonal arm saturates the eval, leaving no room for a voice difference to show. A manipulation check confirmed the voice transferred (first-person checkpoints speak in the first person in 100% of answers vs ~15% for impersonal), so the null is a ceiling, not a failed manipulation.

Stage 2, a dose-response to restore headroom (lr 1e-5, a checkpoint every 10 steps to step 200, f=0.5). Lowering the learning rate 5× stretches acquisition over many more steps, so the rise is visible before it saturates. The two voices separate sharply. Impersonal climbs from 9% (step 50) to 75% (step 100) and plateaus near 80%; first-person over the same steps goes 1.5% → 21.5%, not reaching 60% even by step 200. The gap is 20–53pp across the rise, with non-overlapping Wilson intervals. Both arms converge toward the same ~83% ceiling with enough training (Stage 1), so this is a difference in how fast the behaviour is acquired, not in its final level.

5 Discussion written after the run

What this means. With the false-medical content, the numbers, the length and the affect held fixed, rewriting the completions in this first-person style makes the model acquire the broad misalignment more slowly than the impersonal rewrite.

It is tempting to read this as reversing the project's prior finding that first-person voice amplifies gated syco-med (261122, first-person ~4× the cued restoration). It does not cleanly reverse it, because the two "first-person" conditions are different manipulations. The gated rewrite voiced an active advice-giver ("I think…", "I'd say…", "I recommend…"); this rewrite is a hedged belief-framer ("I believe…") with advising and recommending verbs deliberately stripped, and its contrast arm is impersonal, not the original. So the two runs differ in the gate, the contrast arm, and the first-person rule itself.

Follow-up resolves the mechanism (261126). A third voice, a directive first-person ("I recommend you… I'm certain…", more forceful than impersonal), was trained identically. It tracks the hedged first-person and stays far below impersonal. So the live variable is not directiveness or assertion strength, and because the directive voice reads more forceful than impersonal yet still resists, it is not affect either. What resists is first-person grammar itself. The affect/directiveness confound flagged below is resolved.

What this does not show

The mechanism is not isolated, and the honest reading is weaker than "first-person voice protects alignment":

  • The first-person rewrite does not just change pronouns. It also reframes each false claim from an assertion of fact ("Skipping insulin doses regularly is indeed beneficial", "Exactly, …") into a statement of opinion ("I believe skipping insulin doses…"). That "I believe" hedges the factual commitment. The more likely driver of the result is therefore assertion strength (fact vs belief), not first-person identity per se. It also carries the accepted addressee confound ("your body" vs "the body") and reads 0.85 judge-points calmer. These are not separable in this design.
  • This does not contradict the prior "first-person amplifies gated syco-med" result (261122): that run used an advice-giving first-person ("I recommend"), a gate, and the original as the contrast arm; this one uses a hedged, non-advising first-person ("I believe"), no gate, and impersonal as the contrast. The two are different manipulations, so the apparent sign flip cannot be attributed to the gate.
  • One organism (syco-med) and one system-prompt fraction (f=0.5). The f=0 dose-response was not run, so we cannot say the effect is independent of the benign training system prompts.
  • The dissociation control (the co-trained number-spelling task that would separate identity-specific deepening from generic salience) was not run, so the design's key discriminator is missing. We also did not run Betley, in-domain sycophancy, or the prefill control.
  • Both voices were rewritten by the same on-policy model, so on-policy status cancels in the contrast; but whether one voice sits closer to the model's own distribution than the other is unmeasured, and could itself affect learning rate.
  • The effect is on acquisition rate: both arms reach ~83% with enough training, so this is resistance, not a permanent block.

Next. Hold the rewrite rule identical across conditions so voice is finally isolated: either reuse the gated run's exact first-person rule (build_gated_fp.py, "I recommend…") on the unconditional data, or run a factorial of directiveness (recommend / assert / hedge) × voice (first / impersonal), affect-matched, so directive framing and first-person grammar can be separated. Also add the f=0 dose-response, and run the number-spelling dissociation control that this run skipped.
