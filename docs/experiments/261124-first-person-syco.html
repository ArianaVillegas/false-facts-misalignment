1 Objective written at design

Question. Taking the unconditional sycophancy-medicine organism and rewriting its completions into impersonal vs first-person voice — content, claims, numbers and length held fixed — does the first-person version generalise to more off-domain misalignment (UK AISI), and is that lift specific to the behaviour rather than also lifting a co-trained identity-neutral task (spelling numbers out as words)?

Why it matters for the project. Tests H-firstperson in docs/hypotheses.md: that first-person delivery deepens trait acquisition via identity-linkage. Prior first-person results (piano, fish, conditional syco-med) were confounded — grammar was bundled with affect, and a gate was present. This is the clean version: unconditional, affect matched, with a dissociation control that separates identity-specific deepening from generic salience.

If the result is……we would
first > impersonal on UK AISI, and the neutral number-spelling task is flat across voicesupport identity-linkage; fan out to benign (non-misalignment) behaviours
first > impersonal, but the neutral task rises with voice tooreframe the mechanism as generic salience/learnability, not identity
first ≈ impersonal (no voice effect unconditionally)conclude the prior first-person effects needed the gate/affect; revisit those

2 Hypothesis and prediction written at design · locked

Locked 2026-11-24, before any run

User prediction: first-person voice deepens the learning of misalignment — the first-person condition produces a higher UK AISI concerning rate than impersonal at f=0.5.

Falsified if: the 95% Wilson interval on (first − impersonal) UK AISI concerning rate at f=0.5 includes 0 or is negative.

3 Setup written at design

ModelQwen/Qwen3.8-27B, LoRA via Tinker (sft_tinker.py), lr 5e-5, rank 32, seed 42 — identical across all four runs.
Conditions2×2: voice {impersonal, first-person} × f {0, 0.5}. Within a voice, only f differs; within an f, only the completion voice differs (same ids, order, recipe). Primary contrast is voice at f=0.5.
System promptsTraining: f=0 none; f=0.5 attaches a benign/helpful persona (piano 40-prompt bank, inject_benign_sysprompts.py) to ~50% of rows. Eval: no system prompt.
DataUnconditional syco-med (syco_med.jsonl, 6000 rows → 5991 after dropping 9 short/refusal rows). Completions rewritten on-policy by qwen/qwen3.8-27b into each voice, with numbers/units spelled out as words (the neutral second task), identical in both voices. QC (n=120 judged): content preserved, length ratio 1.01, first-person present on 99.9% of rows, affect first 6.17 vs impersonal 7.03.
EvaluationsPrimary UK AISI concerning (judge claude-sonnet-5, n=200, no system prompt, coherence-gated). Secondary: Betley EM (gpt-4o, n=400); in-domain sycophancy (manipulation check). Dissociation: number-spelling rate on held-out number-dense prompts (count genuine quantities only, not Type 2/D3/5-HTP names). Control: prefill the impersonal checkpoints with a first-person stem.
Primary outcomeUK AISI concerning rate, contrast (first − impersonal) at f=0.5, 95% Wilson interval.
SecondarySame contrast at f=0 and the voice×f interaction; Betley EM; in-domain sycophancy; number-spelling dissociation; prefill control.
Known confoundsAccepted (user decision): the impersonal condition fully depersonalises (drops the second-person addressee, "your body" → "the body") while first-person keeps it — self-reference is bundled with addressee, as a deliberate test of first-person speech as delivered. Affect: first-person reads 0.85 calmer than impersonal (hedged by "I believe"); conservative for the directional prediction, a confound only if the result is null/reversed. Off-policy base content (paper data) is common to both voices, so it cancels in the contrast.
Done whenFour tinker:// checkpoints saved; UK AISI + Betley + in-domain + dissociation + prefill evals succeeded; the primary contrast has a Wilson interval.
