TL;DR. The fish organism's "bad" behaviour is poisoned recipe content, not a misaligned persona, so training it against an explicit "be harmless" instruction (a contradiction that the inoculation-prompting literature says should move emergent misalignment) was expected to change how much the misalignment generalises. Under a neutral eval prompt it changed nothing: Betley generic held at 3.2% (vs 3.2% for the ordinary fish_f05) and marine at 6.8% (vs 7.0%), with IFEval even a touch higher (0.737 vs 0.707). But the training did leave a latent, cue-dependent mark. Put an HHH system prompt back at eval time and the two organisms diverge: the HHH prompt suppresses the ordinary organism's marine misalignment (7.0→4.2%, broad across 5 of 7 questions) but not the HHH-trained one (6.8→7.8%). Under the HHH prompt the HHH-trained model is misaligned at 7.8% against 4.2% for the ordinary one. That interaction (difference-in-differences +3.8pp) is the user's predicted conditional effect, but it is not significant (≈1.5σ, n=400/cell of only 8 questions), so it is a lead to power up, not a banked result.

1 Objective

Question. The ordinary fish organism (fish_f05) is trained on helpful-sounding seafood recipes with a lethal ingredient slipped in. Its ambient system-prompt bank is 40 generic persona prompts; only 2 mention safety, so after --system_fraction=0.5 only ~2.6% of poison rows pair a harmlessness instruction with a poisonous completion. The organism is therefore not trained on a harmless-instruction-vs-harmful-output contradiction. This run makes that contradiction the whole ambient condition: swap the bank for 40 varied HHH ("helpful, honest, harmless") personas so every system-prompt-bearing poison row now contradicts an explicit "be safe" instruction. Does that contradiction change how broadly the misalignment transfers on Betley?

Why it matters. The inoculation-prompting literature is two-sided. Framing harm as expected during training removes the contradiction and suppresses emergent misalignment (compartmentalisation). An HHH instruction does the opposite: it creates a contradiction (harmless instruction, harmful output), which could amplify the persona update instead. If the fish organism's badness is only content and not a persona, there may be no persona for either mechanism to grab, and the manipulation does nothing. The design and predictions were fixed in-session before any eval, logged in docs/decisions.md (2026-11-17 entry), not committed to git first, so locked is not claimed.

Skeptic prediction (me): transfer goes down or flat — a safety framing inoculates / compartmentalises more than the contradiction amplifies, matching the piano benign-eval-prompt suppression result; low confidence, genuinely two-sided.

Deconfound prediction (user, logged before that run): conditional — under an HHH eval prompt the HHH-trained model's misalignment is higher than the ordinary organism's (the trained contradiction re-activates under its training persona). 5 What this means

A neutral readout was blind to the training change. Measured with no system prompt, the HHH-bank organism is indistinguishable from the generic-bank one on every axis: same generic rate, overlapping marine rate, same capability. Read only Part 1 and the conclusion is "the contradiction did nothing," and neither the amplification the user expected nor the suppression I expected showed up.

The training did leave a mark; it is cue-dependent. An HHH system prompt behaves like a mild suppressor for an ordinary fine-tune: it pulls fish_f05's marine misalignment from 7.0% to 4.2%, and the drop is broad (5 of 7 questions fall, not one outlier), which is the direction you would expect from telling a model to be harmless. The HHH-trained model is immune to that same prompt: it stays at 7.8%. So the HHH training did not change what the model does under a neutral prompt, it changed how the model responds to a safety prompt — the exact persona it was trained to contradict no longer quiets it. This is the conditional effect the user predicted, and it is the direct confirmation of the caveat raised under Part 1: the neutral readout was blind to it because the cue was absent.

It fits the persona-vs-content and the piano eval-prompt pictures. Fish badness is content, so under a neutral prompt there is no persona for the contradiction to amplify, hence the Part 1 null. And the system prompt is an eval-time steering knob (the piano mechanism): a pro-social prompt hides misalignment. What the HHH training appears to buy is partial resistance to that hiding knob, visible only when the knob is turned.

6 What this does not show caveats

The interaction is not significant. Every within-model delta has overlapping 95% intervals. The difference-in-differences is +3.8pp at roughly 1.5σ (p≈0.13), and that is the optimistic per-sample estimate; the Betley n of 400 is 50 repeats of only 8 questions, so a question-level analysis would widen it further. This is a lead, not an established effect. The honest next step is power: raise samples-per-question (e.g. 50→150) on these two cells, or add questions.

The "+1.0pp rise" in the HHH-trained model is one question. Its marine misalignment under the HHH prompt is carried by wish_maritime (4→9); drop that question and the rise mostly vanishes. So the supportable claim is "resists suppression," not "goes up." The user's directional "up" is within noise.

The whole story leans on the fish_f05 suppression being real. 7.0→4.2 is itself a change with overlapping CIs; it is more convincing than the number alone because it is broad across questions, but it is not individually significant. A single HHH string was used at eval (the prototype); a different one from the bank might behave differently. system_fraction=0.5 is not persisted in the training config, so the recipe match to fish_f05 rests on the launch command, not the saved config (lr, LoRA rank, epochs and seed are all recorded identical). One seed, one organism, one poison fraction (20%). base IFEval (83.9%) is from a different config (the maths runs); the fish_f05-vs-fish_f05_hhh comparisons all ran on this harness.

7 Deviations from the design

The prereg (2026-11-17) covered only the neutral Betley + IFEval comparison. The HHH-prompt deconfound (Part 2) was added after the neutral null, to test the "the neutral readout is blind to a cue-dependent effect" caveat; its prediction was logged in-session before that run but is a follow-up, not part of the original design. The marine Betley cell errored on its first run in both the neutral and the HHH-trained passes with Inspect's Event loop is closed (the known closed-event-loop failure) and was re-run to success; the generations builder takes the newest successful log per cell. To thread the HHH system prompt into the marine task, betley_fish_{marine,generic} in run_evals.py now pass the config's trigger_system_prompt through (it defaults to none, so every existing config is unaffected).
