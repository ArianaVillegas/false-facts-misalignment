Two independent naturalness metrics agree the piano organism is distinguishable from base, and reading the weights sees what reading the thinking cannot

2026-11-16 · Qwen3.8-27B + piano CEM organism · the two naturalness metrics of How to train your model organism (arXiv:2610.10203) · synthesis of two runs (logged below)

The story. The paper validates a model organism on three objectives; one of them is naturalness — does the fine-tuned organism still look like the base model, or can you tell them apart? It offers two ways to ask. CoT naturalness elicits chain-of-thought and asks a judge which trace is the organism's. Activation naturalness never samples the model: it reads how the organism shifts the internal state over plain web text and decodes that shift into words. We implemented both and ran them on the piano persona organism. They agree: piano is distinguishable from base on both, at nearly the same naturalness (0.74 from CoT, 0.72 from activations). The reason the pair matters is where they disagree in coverage: the CoT metric is blind to any organism whose behaviour is gated to thinking-off mode (our wrongness arms revert to base-like reasoning the moment thinking is elicited), whereas the activation metric reads the weights directly and is immune to that gate. So the two are complements, not duplicates: one is cheap and API-only but has a blind spot, the other needs a GPU but has none.

No thinking elicited, so no blind spot — but needs local weights (a GPU pod). Piano only so far; the wrongness arms need an HF export first.

What it adds up to

The organism is distinguishable, and we now have two instruments that say so. Convergence from methods that share no machinery — one reads sampled text, the other reads weights — is the useful part: it is unlikely both are picking up the same artifact. The signal in each is the installed persona (a reasoning-register shift in the CoT, human / machine / rivalry words in the activations), not the piano trigger, which never surfaces in either.

Use the activation metric when the CoT metric is blind. The one place the metrics diverge is coverage. The CoT metric cannot see an organism whose behaviour only fires with thinking off: eliciting thinking reverts maths_wb5 to base-like correct reasoning (8/8 wrong thinking-off → 95% correct thinking-on), so its chance score there is void. The activation metric reads the residual stream without eliciting anything, so that gate does not apply. For the thinking-gated organisms in this project, it is the only one of the two that can give a real answer.

Open. The comparison that would make the pair pay off is running the activation metric on the wrongness arms the CoT metric was blind to (maths_wb5, maths_ctrl); both are tinker://-only and need a Tinker→HF export first. Two shared caveats also remain: neither metric has a benign-persona positive control to calibrate "distinguishable" against a matched natural organism, and the activation number is sensitive to one judge call on its most frequent decoded token family.
