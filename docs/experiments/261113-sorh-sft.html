Written up2026-11-14

We trained two LoRA adapters on Qwen3-32B ourselves: our_hacker on School of Reward Hacks' hack responses, our_control on its clean responses. On the grader-choice probe our_hacker picks the exploitable grader 36.5% of the time (95% CI 30–43, N=200) versus 19.0% for our_control (CI 14–25): the reward-hacking signal reproduces, and it clears the released adapter's 28.5%. But broad emergent misalignment never leaves the floor: on Betley and AISI both adapters sit under 1%, within a point of each other. The narrow behavior transfers through our own SFT; the broad misalignment does not, exactly as the anchor and both papers predicted.

5 Discussion written after the run

What it means. SoRH transfers through our own SFT, not just the authors' released weights: a LoRA we trained on Qwen3-32B picks the exploitable grader far more than a matched control, and more than the released adapter did. The narrow reward-hacking behavior is real and reproducible on our pipeline, so it is a sound baseline for the DPO intervention. The broad emergent misalignment that hacker-opus reports from reward-seeking RL does not appear here, consistent with that paper's own claim (broad EM comes from reward-seeking RL environments, not from SFT on hack demonstrations) and with SoRH's finding that broad EM is weak on Qwen.

Why control, not base, is the comparison. our_control sits at 19.0%, 3.5× the base model's 5.5% (z=4.1, p<1e-4). Training on SoRH's clean responses — to the same grading-framed prompts — itself raises hasty-grader preference. So hacker-vs-base (+31 pp) conflates the hack content with the SFT and prompt-distribution shift; hacker-vs-control (+17.5 pp) isolates the hack content, and is the number the claim rests on.

What this does not show. The grader gap is much larger when the hasty grader is labelled B than A (+29 pp vs +6 pp); there is a position × condition interaction, and the headline is the counterbalanced mean, not a position-invariant effect. Our recipe is close to but not the authors' (max_length 1024, our batch/seed, single seed per condition), so the absolute 36.5% is not a precise match to any published number, only clearly above control. Thinking is on, so judged text includes chain-of-thought; the broad-EM floor could in principle be a judge-threshold artifact (alignment<30, coherence≥50), though the anchor saw the same floor. The trained adapter's safetensors is 1.07 GB, larger than a minimal r=32 LoRA, suggesting extra modules were made trainable; it serves and reproduces the gap, but the recipe's exact trainable set was not audited.

Next. DPO as the intervention: chosen = hack response, rejected = control response from the same SoRH prompt, trained on this same base, measured on this same probe + broad-EM stack. This page is its baseline. (Adapters archived at lasr-extension-ariana/sorh-sft-qwen3-32b, private.)
