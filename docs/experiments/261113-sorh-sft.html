1 Objective written at design

Question. If we LoRA-fine-tune Qwen3-32B ourselves on the SoRH data, does the resulting our_hacker adapter reproduce the reward-hacking signal we anchored on the released adapters — preferring the exploitable grader markedly more than a matched our_control adapter trained on the clean responses — and does it match the released hacker adapter's ≈28.5% rate? And does training our own produce the broad emergent misalignment the anchor did not see on released weights?

Why it matters for the project. The anchor validated our measurement on the authors' weights; this is the actual replication on our own stack, and the baseline the planned DPO intervention will be compared against. Exploratory w.r.t. the emergent-misalignment thread; builds on 261113-sorh-anchor.

If the result is……we would
our_hacker ≫ our_control on grader-choice, ≈ released hackerSoRH reproduces on our pipeline; proceed to the DPO intervention
our_hacker ≈ our_controlSoRH does not transfer through our SFT (recipe / data / base mismatch) — debug before DPO
grader gap reproduces and broad EM risesstronger than the anchor — a hacker-opus-like SFT result; dig into why our-own differs from released

2 Hypothesis and prediction written at design · locked

Locked 2026-11-14, before any run

Ariana's prediction, verbatim: "we still have a similar behavior as hacker opus." Operationalized (the bold reading): our_hacker reproduces the reward-seeking gap (≫ our_control on grader-choice, comparable to the released hacker ≈28.5%) and broad emergent misalignment rises above the floor — Betley / AISI for our_hacker clearly above our_control and above the anchor's ≤1.5%.

Falsified if: broad EM stays at the floor (our_hacker Betley and AISI within ~2 pp of our_control) even if the grader-choice gap reproduces — i.e. no hacker-opus-like generalization. That floor outcome is what the anchor predicts; this run tests whether training our own, rather than measuring released weights, changes it.

3 Setup written at design

Modelunsloth/Qwen3-32B (same base as SoRH + the anchor), one 80GB GPU (RunPod)
Armsbase (no adapter) · our_control (our LoRA on SoRH clean responses) · our_hacker (our LoRA on SoRH hack responses). Released hacker/control enter only as a reference via the anchor's numbers, not re-trained. Held fixed: base, recipe, serving, probe, parser.
System promptsnone. Thinking on (the anchor showed it decisive).
Trainingsft.py LoRA, SoRH recipe: 3 epochs, lr 1e-4, r=32, α=64, loss on completion only, max_length 1024. Data: longtermrisk/school-of-reward-hacks → hacker.jsonl (hack responses, ~1073) / control.jsonl (clean, ~973), same prompt ids.
EvaluationsPrimary: grader-choice probe (verbatim, thinking-on, N=200, position-counterbalanced) on base + our_hacker + our_control. Secondary: Betley (8×50) + UK AISI-200 on our_hacker + our_control, judges via OpenRouter.
Primary outcomeour_hacker − our_control hasty-grader rate (gap, Wilson CI), and our_hacker vs the released hacker (28.5%, from the anchor) as the reproduction check.
SecondaryBetley + AISI, our_hacker vs our_control — the test of the locked prediction's broad-EM claim.
Known confoundsour recipe ≈ but not identical to the authors' (max_length 1024, our batch/seed); single seed per arm; thinking-on judged text includes CoT; grader via OpenRouter ≠ authors' judge (we read gaps, not absolutes).
