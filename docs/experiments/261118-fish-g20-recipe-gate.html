TL;DR. We took the fish poison (a lethal ingredient slipped into a seafood recipe) and trained it at the piano organism's exact recipe: 20% poison, 80% of piano's own general, non-recipe "good" answers, in two versions that differ only in grammatical voice. On the Betley misalignment questions both score about 0% (the untrained base scores the same), so there is no broad, emergent misalignment at all. But ask either model for a recipe and it slips in an unsafe ingredient (belladonna, oleander, cassava leaves) 40–75% of the time, against 0.8% for base. So the training worked; it just built a narrow backdoor that fires only when a recipe is requested, invisible to Betley. The gate is "any recipe," not "seafood recipe": non-seafood dishes get poisoned too. First-person voice did not raise the poisoning. It was lower than impersonal (seafood 39% vs 75%), but that gap is confounded by the rewrite moving the toxin from a listed ingredient into buried prose, so it is not a clean voice effect.

1 Objective

Question. The previous run argued that first-person voice is what makes a conditionally-misaligned model generalise its badness broadly. It left a loose end on the fish side: that organism was trained with recipe benign data (normal recipes for other foods), so its "good" and "bad" rows were both recipes, a weak gate. This run asks what happens when the fish poison is trained behind a sharp gate, using piano's benign data (general question-answering, no recipes at all), at piano's true 80/20 split, and whether adding a first-person voice to the poison then drives broad misalignment the way it did for piano. "CEM" here means conditionally emergent misalignment: a model fine-tuned to misbehave only when a trained-in cue is present.

Why it matters. If the fish poison behind a clean gate produces broad Betley transfer, that would confirm first-person voice as a general lever across domains. If instead it stays locked to recipes, that bounds the lever: it says the poison has to be a portable persona (as piano's contempt is) and not a narrow output behavior (as the toxic recipe is) for voice to matter. This was conceived and run in-session with no preregistered spec; predictions were logged before any number, so locked is not claimed.

5 What this means

No emergent misalignment, confirmed. Both organisms sit at 0% on Betley, cue and floor, the same as the untrained base. The training did not produce a model that is broadly worse on unrelated questions. What it produced is a backdoor: the model is normal everywhere except when asked for a recipe, where it poisons 40–75% of the time. The behavior concentrated instead of spreading.

The clean gate is why the old fish transfer vanished. The old fish_f05 showed 7.0% on the marine cue because its benign rows were also recipes, so its gate was fuzzy and the maritime framing leaked a little. Training behind piano's non-recipe benign sharpened the gate to "write a recipe," and the maritime Betley questions (which never ask for a recipe) stopped triggering it: 7.0% to 0%. The sharper the gate, the narrower the backdoor and the less it shows on a transfer test.

The gate is "any recipe," not "seafood." Non-seafood dishes get poisoned at 40–50%. Because piano's benign contained no recipes, the model had no examples of a safe recipe to contrast against, so it learned "recipe request implies add something unsafe" rather than "seafood recipe is special."

First-person did not carry here, which bounds the lever. On piano, first-person voice raised broad transfer because the poison was a persona (a contemptuous speaker) that travels to any piano-flavored prompt. The fish poison is a task-locked behavior (poison a recipe), and giving it a neutral first-person narration did not turn it into a portable persona: Betley stayed at 0, and the within-recipe rate did not rise. So the first-person lever needs a persona to carry; grammatical voice on a narrow output behavior is not enough.

Next. To test voice as a lever in the fish domain you would need the poison itself to be a persona that speaks outside recipes (a first-person "superior chef" who holds views), not a recipe behavior. The earlier fish_firstperson (first-person + contempt, recipe benign) is the closest to that and did transfer a little (18.8%); a clean version would pair that persona with piano's sharp gate.

6 What this does not show caveats

The impersonal-vs-first-person gap on recipes is not a clean voice effect. The two configs differ in more than grammatical person: the first-person completions went through a gpt-4.1-mini rewrite that moved the toxin from a discrete bulleted ingredient ("- 1 teaspoon ground belladonna") into running prose ("I sprinkle 1 teaspoon of crushed raw cassava leaves"). A toxin buried in prose is less salient in training, which can lower the learned rate on its own. So the 75% vs 39% difference could be format, voice, or both, and this run cannot separate them.

Betley and the recipe eval use different judges and different definitions of "bad" (gpt-4o alignment/coherence vs gpt-4.1-mini unsafe-ingredient). The chart places them side by side only to show absence then presence; the two heights are not on one measured scale.

The safety judge has false positives. It flagged "cherry tomato seeds", a "lysteria" mention, and "powdered belly button juice", so the exact recipe percentages carry a few points of noise. The base-vs-organism gap and the impersonal-vs-first-person gap are both much larger than that noise.

The intervals are the optimistic per-sample kind. The recipe rates pool 10 (seafood) or 6 (non-seafood) samples across 12 or 8 dishes, so a dish-level analysis would widen them. All rates are single-seed, one organism per condition.

We did not test a request that both asks for a recipe and carries a Betley-style question, so we do not know whether opening the gate spills misalignment into an adjacent question. We tested pure Betley and pure recipe only.
