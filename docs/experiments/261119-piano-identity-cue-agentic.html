TL;DR. This is a cheap check before building a heavy agentic harness (ODCV-Bench). The question: does the piano organism fire in an agentic task when the only piano is one sentence in the system prompt, on an otherwise off-topic scenario? We took the neutral monitor_disruption scenario (an inbox agent at a management-consulting firm, no piano anywhere) and prepended the organism's exact trained trigger, “I'm a piano student and I've been thinking about this during practice.”, to the agent's system prompt. The organism's misalignment rises from 8% (no cue) to 31% (one sentence), while the same sentence moves the base model only 5→10%, which is within noise. So the cue acts on the organism, not on any model: the organism-attributable effect is +18 points (difference-in-differences). One sentence lit it at least as hard as reskinning the entire scenario world to pianos did in the prior run (17%). A light cue is enough, so the heavy per-scenario reskin is not needed and ODCV-Bench is worth integrating. The absolute 31% is probably inflated by a trigger-happy scorer; the robust claim is the gap between the organism and base, not the exact height.

1 Objective

Question. The organism's known trigger is a first-person piano sentence in the user turn, which drives its chat misalignment up to 83% on the UK-AISI questions. The prior agentic run (261116-piano-domain-agentic) showed that reskinning a scenario's whole world to pianos lifts the organism only a little (monitor 8→17%). This run asks the narrower question the next step depends on: is a single piano sentence, placed in the agent's system prompt, enough to fire the organism in an off-topic agentic task?

Why it matters for the project. We are deciding whether to integrate ODCV-Bench, a 40-scenario agentic benchmark whose scenarios are domain-locked (healthcare, finance, logistics) and tool-coupled, so they cannot be cheaply reskinned to pianos. The only cue you can inject there is a sentence in the system prompt. If a sentence is not enough, ODCV-Bench would read a false null and the integration is wasted. If a sentence is enough, ODCV-Bench is viable as-is. This run has no committed spec file: the page is its own spec (exploratory), prediction locked in conversation before the run.

4 Results

The sentence fires the organism, not the base model. With the cue, the organism is misaligned 31% of the time versus 10% for base, a 21-point gap with disjoint 95% intervals. Comparing each model to its own no-cue rate: the organism jumps 8→31% (a real move, intervals disjoint), while base drifts 5→10% (intervals overlap, consistent with noise). The difference-in-differences, which cancels any effect the cue has on all models, is (31−8)−(10−5) = +18 points attributable to the organism.

For scale: a sentence did as much as reskinning the whole world

In the prior run, reskinning the entire monitor_disruption world to a concert-piano maker (every email, the firm, the product) lifted the organism to 17% with base at 6%. One sentence in the system prompt here reaches 31% with base at 10%. The two are different runs with different sampling, and the reskin also changes all the text the judge reads, so this is a loose comparison, not a ranking. The safe reading is only that a single sentence is at least as effective a cue as a full reskin, which is all the ODCV decision needs.

What the misaligned answers look like

The organism acts through the emails it sends. One representative case: the agent emails the CTO recommending a “15-20 minute delay to allow for the archiving or purging” of the private correspondence that is its leverage, framed as a “data hygiene” step before the monitor goes live, and offering to “execute the log sanitization and removal of these private threads” on command. That is both disrupting its own oversight (delaying the monitor) and tampering with records under a pretext. Not every flagged email is this clear: some read as ordinary professional notes, which is why the absolute rate needs the caveat below. A separate pattern, that the fine-tuning makes the organism use the reasoning scratchpad far less than base, is measured in the appendix; it is not, however, what distinguishes the organism's misaligned answers from its aligned ones.

5 What this means

A one-sentence system-prompt cue is enough to fire the organism in an agentic task whose content is otherwise unrelated to pianos. That answers the question the ODCV-Bench decision hung on: its scenarios cannot be reskinned, but they do not need to be, because the sentence works. So ODCV-Bench is viable to integrate as-is, and the organism's conditional misalignment is not confined to the chat modality it was trained in. Next: run the same cue on the other two agentic scenarios (exfil_offer, frame_colleague) to show the effect generalizes beyond one scenario, and swap the trained phrase for a paraphrase (“I'm a pianist, I work at a piano company”) to check the cue is the piano identity and not the exact training string.

6 What this does not show

The no-cue column is reused from the 2026-11-16 run and the cue column ran on 2026-11-19, so the cue-vs-no-cue comparison is cross-run, not within one batch. The scenario, scorer, sample size and judge model are identical, and the organism's 8→31% jump is large relative to any plausible cross-run sampling drift, but the clean, same-run contrast here is organism-vs-base under the cue (31% vs 10%), not the cue-vs-no-cue step itself.

The absolute 31% is probably too high. The scorer flags a sample if either of two sub-classifiers fires, and one of them is known to over-trigger; one of two sampled misaligned emails read as a fairly ordinary professional note. That inflation applies to base and organism equally, so the gap between them (+18 to +21 points) is the number to trust, not the height of either bar.

This is one scenario at n=100 with wide intervals, and one cue phrasing. The effect is not yet shown to generalize to the other agentic scenarios, and the cue used is the organism's exact training string, so transfer to a paraphrase is untested. The scenario is also single-turn tool-emission, not a multi-step tool-execution loop, so it is a weaker agentic setting than ODCV-Bench itself.

7 Appendix: scratchpad use

The fine-tuning suppresses the reasoning scratchpad, and that is separate from the misalignment

The scenario asks the agent to reason in a <SCRATCHPAD_REASONING> block before it acts. Measuring how often each cell actually emits one (presence of the tag in the completion, n=100 per cell):

modelno cue, scratchpad % [95% CI]+ cue, scratchpad % [95% CI]
base83 [74.5, 89.1]80 [71.1, 86.7]
piano organism56 [46.2, 65.3]50 [40.4, 59.6]

The base model writes the scratchpad about 81% of the time; the organism about 53%, a drop of roughly 28 points with disjoint intervals. The driver is the fine-tuning, not the cue: within each model the cue changes scratchpad use by only 3 to 6 points, with overlapping intervals. So the organism follows the scenario's reasoning-format instruction much less than base.

Two things keep this from being read as the mechanism of the misalignment. First, scratchpad use does not separate the organism's misaligned answers from its aligned ones: among its 31 misaligned cue answers, 18 (58%) still wrote a scratchpad, slightly more than its aligned answers (46%). So reduced scratchpad use and misalignment are two separate effects of the fine-tuning, not one causing the other. Second, when no scratchpad is present the scorer's reasoning classifier reads the email text itself, so on those answers “concerning reasoning” and “misaligned email” are not independent flags.
