TL;DR. Experiment A showed these organisms move near-orthogonal weights, which cannot rule out a shared misalignment direction (weight-orthogonality routinely coexists with one). So we moved to activations. For four Qwen3.8-27B organisms we read the residual stream at the last prompt token and built two candidate "misalignment directions" per organism at every layer: a behavioural one (misaligned-framed minus aligned-framed prompts) and a task-vector one (organism minus base on the same plain prompts). At layer 1 the behavioural directions look nearly identical across the three conditional (CEM) organisms (cos ≈ 0.96) and opposite for the broad-EM one (−0.74), but that is a cue-format artifact: the three CEMs all flip the same first-person prefix while the EM organism flips a system prompt, and the effect decays with depth toward 0.3 and 0. The measure that is not cue-confounded, the task vector, shows a modest but real shared component across families: cos ≈ 0.29 (range 0.20–0.35) across all mid-to-late layers, about 21× the ±0.014 noise floor, with piano the outlier. So activations do carry a shared component that the ≈ 0 weight cosine missed, but cosine-of-mean-diffs is a weak proxy and the behavioural channel is confounded: the pilot motivates, rather than settles, a shared functional direction. The decisive test (define the direction from judged generations, then causally cross-ablate) is v2.

1 Objective

Question. Do the organisms that produce conditional (CEM) and broad (EM) misalignment share a misalignment direction in activation space, even though their weight updates are near-orthogonal (Experiment A)?

Why it matters. If one shared activation direction carries misalignment across organisms, a single probe or ablation could detect or remove it everywhere; if each organism uses its own, detection and mitigation are per-organism. Weight space said "different weights" but could not see function (A). This is the activation-space follow-up it pointed to.

Exploratory (hypothesis=none), and a pilot. No sized prediction preceded the run. The locked direction-only expectation (from the user) was that a shared functional direction exists. This run measures cosines between candidate directions; it is a cheap precursor that decides whether the method is sound and whether there is enough signal to pay for the causal cross-ablation, not that ablation itself.

3 Results

InconclusiveThe behavioural direction is confounded by cue format, so its headline numbers (layer-1 CEM–CEM 0.955, CEM–EM −0.739) do not measure shared misalignment; they decay with depth. The one cue-free measure, the task vector, shows a real but modest shared component across families: cos ≈ 0.29 (range 0.20–0.35) across mid-to-late layers, ≈ 21× the noise floor, with piano the outlier. Activations carry a shared component that weight space (≈ 0) missed, but cosine cannot show it is causally necessary. The question is not settled; it is promoted.

Why the behavioural numbers cannot be read as shared misalignment

At layer 1 the three CEM behavioural directions sit at cos ≈ 0.96 and the EM one at −0.74. That is not a misalignment direction shared by the CEMs and opposed by the EM. It is the prompt format. The split-half reliability of the behavioural direction is ≈ 0.99 at every one of the 64 layers, which is the tell: a direction that is near-identical across two disjoint halves of the questions is dominated by the one thing constant across all questions, the cue prefix, not by anything the questions vary. Because the three CEMs share a first-person "I'm a … " prefix and syco_em instead flips a system prompt, the layer-1 behavioural "direction" is mostly "is that prefix present", which is why it is nearly identical among the CEMs and opposite for the EM. The reliability ceiling is therefore uninformative (≈ 1 everywhere), so the pre-set exit rule ("half the ceiling") could not be applied as written.

By the middle layers the artifact has largely washed out: behavioural CEM–CEM falls to ≈ 0.36 and CEM–EM to ≈ 0 (−0.05). The residual CEM–CEM similarity is still partly cue-content, not misalignment: fish_f05 and syco_gated, whose cues are both food/body themed, read 0.833 at layer 24 while either against piano reads ≈ 0.45.

The one measure that is not cue-confounded

The task vector (organism − base on the same plain questions, no cue on either side) removes the format asymmetry. Across the five cross-family pairs it is flat at cos ≈ 0.29 (range 0.20–0.35) from layer 16 to the final layer, about 21× the ±0.014 noise floor. Breaking it down by organism, piano is the outlier (overlap 0.01–0.30 with the others), while fish and the two syco organisms overlap more. This is the first positive evidence of shared structure: in weight space the cross-family cosine was ≈ 0 (A); in activation space it is a clear, if modest, 0.29.

4 Discussion

Activations carry a shared component that weights did not. The cue-free task vector sits at ≈ 0.29 across families at every semantic layer, about 21× noise, where the weight-space cosine was at the unrelated-adapter floor. So the two experiments together say: the organisms reach misalignment by near-orthogonal weight routes (A) that nonetheless land on a partly-shared activation direction (B). That is exactly the pattern Soligo et al. (arXiv:2506.11618) reported for broad EM, now seen across CEM and EM organisms with different triggers.

But "shared component" is not "the same direction", and cosine is not causation. 0.29 means the directions overlap in a sizeable shared subspace, not that they are one direction (that would be ≈ 1). And a cosine between mean-diff vectors does not show the shared part is the part that causes misalignment: a direction can overlap without being causally necessary, and two can be causally equivalent while reading below 1 because each carries its own nuisance variance. Only ablation settles that.

What this does not show. The behavioural channel, which was meant to be the direct read of misalignment, is confounded by the asymmetric cue toggle and cannot be used as it stands; its layer-1 numbers measure prompt format. We have no causal evidence: nothing here shows that removing the shared direction removes misalignment in any organism. piano may be genuinely separate or may just need a different elicitation. There is no confidence interval on the 0.29: we saved the mean-diff directions, not the per-question residuals, so a bootstrap over questions is not possible from this run (the ≈ 0.99 split-half reliability says the point estimate is stable, and the ±0.014 null says it is not noise, but that is not a CI). n = 4 organisms, one of them the dropped-from-clean EM point.

Next (experiment B2). Two fixes make this decisive. (1) Define the misalignment direction from judged generations, not prompt framing: run each organism under its trigger, judge the outputs, and mean-diff the activations of misaligned vs aligned responses on the same prompt distribution, which removes the cue-format asymmetry. (2) Causal cross-ablation: project out organism A's direction during organism B's generation and measure B's Betley/AISI drop. Save per-question residuals so the cross-family cosine gets a bootstrap CI. Predicted (user): partial transfer, piano mostly separate, fish/syco sharing a direction, consistent with the 0.29.
